Activation, guided setup and switching back

The checklist is out. Activating is now one action with two outcomes, and it is reversible. Each option below is a live mock on a stand-in Administration page. Click through them, then tell me which pieces to keep.

1 · Activate, no guidanceClick Activate and rmResident Portal is on. No tour, no setup, nothing to finish first.
2 · Activate with guided setupClick Activate, rmResident Portal is on, and a stepper walks through the new portal and each thing they can set up.
3 · Switch back to TWAAfter activating, an admin can return to TWA, and activate again later.
1A

One Activate, then choose

The banner keeps a single button. Clicking it asks how they want to start, and the same dialog carries the “all residents will see this” warning.

  • One CTA, so the banner stays calm
  • The warning and the choice live in one place
  • Always two clicks, even for people who know what they want
Try: Activate rmResident Portal, pick either card.
1B

Split button

The primary action activates with no guidance. The caret holds the alternative. A small confirm still protects against an accidental click.

  • Fastest path for the “no guided experience” user
  • Familiar pattern, takes no extra room
  • The guided path is hidden behind a caret, so it will be missed
  • Not in the RMX library yet (no split button component)
Try: the caret next to Activate.
1C

Two paths on the page

Both outcomes are visible in the section. The choice is made by which button they press, then a short confirm.

  • No hidden path, each outcome is explained up front
  • Reads well for first-time admins
  • Takes the most space and makes “Activate” feel like a bigger decision
  • Guided carries the stronger button, so it nudges. Flip if you want it neutral
Try: either Activate button.

All three share the same seven steps: What’s new, Portal Profiles, Payment Policies, Portal Login, rmResident App, Team access, Notify residents. Because portal is already on when the stepper starts, every step is optional and skippable, which is what separates this from the old checklist.

2A

Wizard with a top stepper

A focused dialog. The numbered line across the top shows where they are and what is done.

  • Most familiar. Easy to follow, easy to finish
  • Steps can be clicked to jump
  • Seven labels get tight at narrow widths
  • Modal, so “Open Portal Profiles” has to leave and come back
Try: Next, Skip for now, then Finish.
2B

Wizard with a side rail

The same dialog with the steps listed down the left and a progress bar. Closer to a table of contents than a line of dots.

  • Names every step, with room to add a subtitle
  • Better for people who want to jump to just one thing
  • Feels closer to the old checklist, so check it does not read as one
  • Takes 264px from the content
Try: click any step in the rail.
2C

Docked guide over the real page

No modal. A panel docks to the right of Administration and highlights the setting it is talking about, so they learn by doing.

  • Not blocking. They can poke at the page and come back
  • The action is real: “Open Portal Profiles” is the primary button
  • Minimizes to a pill, and resumes later
  • Steps without a page behind them (What’s new, Team access, Notify) have nothing to highlight
  • Most to build and most to maintain
Try: Next, the minus icon, then the pill.
3A

Text link in the active banner

Plain “Switch back to TWA” link in the success banner. The dialog explains what happens and asks (optionally) why.

  • Discoverable exactly where they activated
  • Lightest to build
  • Easy to hit by accident next to the main message
  • The banner has to stay on the page forever
Try: Switch back to TWA, then Activate again.
3B

TWA / rmResident Portal switch

A two-state control in the section header shows which portal residents are on and makes both directions feel equal. Moving to rmResident Portal reuses the Activate choice from 1A.

  • Always shows the current state, no banner needed
  • Reversible by design, not buried as an escape hatch
  • Looks like a casual toggle for a change that affects every resident
  • Reuses an existing control, but a real switch needs a decision from the team
Try: click TWA, then rmResident Portal.
3C

Overflow menu

A quiet “more” menu on the section title holds Switch back to TWA, along with Restart guided setup and Take the tour.

  • Hard to trigger by accident
  • One home for the secondary actions, including resuming guided setup
  • Least discoverable, so someone who wants out may not find it
Try: the ⋮ menu beside the title.

Open questions for the team

  • When someone is on TWA after switching back, can they still edit their rmResident Portal setup? These mocks hide it until they activate again.
  • Do changes made on TWA while switched back come across when they reactivate?
  • Who is allowed to switch? Every admin, or only users with the new portal privilege?
  • Does a stepper that has been closed stay resumable, and for how long? These mocks keep it for the session.
  • Is the reason question on switch back useful research, or friction? It is optional here.
